# Morrow Under Tow · director’s report

## What worked

The opening take gave us the premise in one frame. The sun fills the left side, the tug fills the right, and the rope crosses the space between them. I put the first sound three frames in. The viewer gets to discover what the boat is towing before the sister explains anything.

The route choice took two shots. The wheelhouse gives the turn to the player’s hand. From above, the white wake draws a curve around the orchard toward the blue arch. That second angle also gives the sister’s warning a destination. Its meaning survives a muted viewing.

The payoff got smaller and better. On the harbour steps, a sheet of ice slips free and gold water runs down the stone. At the bakery, the floury hand withdraws and the plum sleeve holds the bread alone. I kept that release in frame before cutting to the title. The card gets 124 frames, enough time to read the date and price after the question.

## What I would change

The winch remains the weakest link. Both second-round lowering takes put two glowing objects in view. I used the first-round downstroke and cut to the single sun low inside the arch. The edit suggests what the winch does. We never actually see it pull that sun down. I would spend the next winch take on a locked view of the sphere descending past a fixed iron rail, with the drum out of sight. We already have the hand turning it.

I would also put the thaw camera at the stair from the outset. The wide take has the best spreading fissure, but the shopfronts carry lettering. The close take lets us see the landing become usable. The bakery handoff has its own continuity cost. The sister says “heel” while the skipper receives a round loaf. I chose the cleaner exchange of hands. Next time I would ask for one cut face of bread pointing straight into the lens and keep the rest of the loaf inside the bakery.

## How I chose the music without hearing it

I selected **M2**, the clay-cup and bowed-bass order. Four submissions returned five files. I compared all five numerically, aligning each file’s first detected downbeat with the opening frame. I decoded a mono copy at 12 kHz for measurement and calculated mean-square level in the same three picture windows. The table gives RMS in dBFS before mixing. Differences *within* a row show whether that track leaves room for the warning, builds at the arch and eases back at the sill.

| File | Wheelhouse, frames 67–134 | Arch, frames 270–359 | Bread, frames 483–595 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| M1 | −25.3 | −23.7 | −21.6 |
| **M2** | **−28.2** | **−16.6** | **−20.0** |
| M3 a | −29.9 | −31.4 | −19.9 |
| M3 b | −22.3 | −21.4 | −17.6 |
| M4 | −19.7 | −16.2 | −15.6 |

M2 rises **11.6 dB** between the warning and the arch, then falls **3.4 dB** for the bread. That gives the two lines different settings before any dialogue ducking. The requested clay cups suggested a small, hand-played sound suited to the model harbour. That description came from the order. I did not hear the delivered instrumentation, harmony or performance.

Briefcut detected roughly **128.09 BPM** against the requested 120 and placed M2’s first downbeat at **1.289544 s**. I used the next 30 seconds as a single passage. All seven picture cuts land within 19 ms of the detected grid with the source clips at original speed. The score continues under the card and fades over its final 0.30 s.

## Handoff

I validated the written cut using briefcut 0.1.0’s parser, clip discovery, local audio provider and frame-placement code. All seven source trims fit the delivered 1920×1080, 24 fps clips. All twelve local audio assets resolve. All ten spot cues land on their specified master frames. The EDL totals **720 frames at 24 fps**, including the end card. The desk ledger closes at 400 video credits, 3,000 sound and voice credits, and four music attempts.
