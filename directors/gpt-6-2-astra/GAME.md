# Morrow Under Tow

The sun came loose. These days it floats about ten feet above the stern of your little green tug, pulling at its rope. You skipper the only daylight service in a saltwater valley where half the streets are canals. From the wheelhouse you can see the orchard, the ferry steps and your sister's bakery, each waiting for the same morning. Take any channel. Where you tie up, ice turns to navigable water, people get to work and somebody sets a place for you. Eventually you have to cast off again.

## What you can do

- **Steer by landmarks.** Explore the connected valley from the helm. Cut through a drowned orchard at high tide or follow a split brick chimney home. Learn the shortcuts well enough to run them in the dark.
- **Work the sunline.** The sun pulls upward like a buoy under water. Wind the stern winch to bring it low enough to clear a bridge or melt a slot through harbour ice. Pay out rope to throw light over the roofs.
- **Give a town your daylight.** Moor at the ferry steps and the crossing runs. Moor at the orchard and neighbours pick the salt pears. Work continues for as long as you stay. Cargo loaded and people ferried remain where you brought them, so each day's route changes tomorrow's errands.
